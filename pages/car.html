How to Acquire Cars in FH6

There are five primary ways to add vehicles to your garage in Forza Horizon 6 Japan. Understanding each path helps you prioritize your credits and time.

🏪 Autoshow Purchase

Browse and buy cars instantly with credits. Prices range from ₵35,000 for common rides to ₵15,000,000+ for rare hypercars. Rotating daily deals offer significant discounts.

🔍 Barn Finds

Hidden throughout the Japan map are 12 barns containing rare and classic vehicles. Drive near the marked location and complete a discovery challenge to unlock. Restoration cost is separate.

🏆 Season Rewards

Complete seasonal championships and trials to earn exclusive vehicles. These often include special liveries, event-only cars, and collector edition models unavailable anywhere else.

📦 Car Pass

Premium subscribers receive 2-4 cars monthly delivered directly to their garage. Includes exclusive editions, early-access launches, and rare imported models.

Best Cars by Racing Discipline

Based on performance data and community consensus — these are the standout choices for each category.

Street Racing

Street races in Tokyo and Osaka favor cars with balanced handling and strong mid-range power. AWD cars dominate on tight urban circuits while RWD excels on wider boulevards.

  • Nissan GT-R Nismo — The ultimate all-round street performer. Incredible grip from the advanced AWD system, monster power in the 600-720hp range, and planted handling at any speed. Best for players who want one car to rule them all.
  • Honda NSX Type R — Mid-engined balance at its finest. Lighter than the GT-R with similar power makes it a missile on technical circuits. S-tier for pilots who can exploit the rear-mid engine placement.
  • BMW M4 Competition — RWD purity on the street. Excellent power-to-weight, predictable slides, and gorgeous balanced chassis. Great learning car for moving up to the harder S-tier machinery.
  • Honda Civic Type R — FWD doesn't mean boring. The CTR is a weapon on tight city circuits where its compact size and aggressive differential shine. Underrated choice at A-tier pricing.

Drift & Touge

Mountain touge and drift events reward cars with strong rear-wheel-drive dynamics, good weight distribution, and adjustable power. Lightness matters more than outright horsepower here.

  • Toyota AE86 Sprinter Trueno — The legendary Initial D machine. Rear-wheel-drive, lightweight, and incredibly balanced. The 150-300hp range sounds modest but it dominates touge because the fundamentals are perfect.
  • Nissan Silvia S15 — The benchmark drift car. SR20DET engine provides excellent boost response, rear-wheel-drive handling is predictable at the limit, and parts availability is unmatched for builds.
  • Mazda RX-7 FD — The apex predator of high-angle drift. The 13B rotary delivers smooth, linear power and the lightweight body rotates beautifully. High skill ceiling but devastating at full send.
  • Nissan 370Z — Underrated entry point into competitive drift. VQ37VHR engine provides solid torque, the chassis is predictable, and upgrade potential is massive. Great first drift car.

Rally & Off-Road

Japan's varied terrain demands AWD systems, decent ground clearance, and suspension that can handle everything from smooth gravel to rocky mountain trails.

  • Subaru WRX STI Rally Car — The rally icon earns its reputation. 550-650hp, classic rally tuned AWD, and the distinctive rumble of the EJ25 powerplant. Dominates mixed-surface events.
  • Toyota GR Yaris — Compact, lightweight, and brutally effective. The 260-350hp range sounds modest but the all-wheel-drive system and rally-tuned chassis make it feel much faster. Best value rally car in the game.
  • Mitsubishi Lancer Evo X — The final Evo is still a force. Super-all-wheel-control system provides precise torque vectoring, the 400-550hp range is strong, and the sedan's practical shape hides pure competition hardware.
  • Ford F-150 Raptor — Desert terrain belongs to the Raptor. The 450-700hp supercharged V6 handles whoops with composure, the long-travel suspension soaks up anything, and the wide track makes it stable at high speeds.

Hypercar & Exotics

Top speed events, time trials, and show-off cruising. These cars exist at the absolute limit of automotive engineering — S-tier only.

  • Bugatti Chiron — 1479-1600hp of fury. The Chiron defines what a hypercar should be. Best for speed trap events and top speed runs where its monstrous power overwhelms everything else.
  • Porsche 911 GT3 RS — The track-day benchmark. 518-560hp sounds modest next to the Chiron, but the RS's lightweight chassis and aggressive aero make it the fastest car on technical circuits.
  • Koenigsegg Jesko — Absorbs track time like nothing else. The 1280-1600hp from the tri-barrel V8 is accessible and tunable, the rear-wheel-drive setup rewards skilled drivers, and the Jesko Absolut is built for terminal velocity.
  • Lamborghini Revuelto — The plug-in hybrid supercar that actually delivers. 1015+hp combined system output, AWD for instant traction, and the aggressive Lamborghini character in a package that corners as well as it accelerates.

New Player Car Recommendations

Starting out in FH6 Japan with limited credits? These three buys give you maximum versatility across the most content.

  1. Toyota Supra GR (A-tier, ~₵180,000) — The perfect first serious car. RWD handling teaches proper technique, the 382-510hp range is accessible on any surface, and the Toyota tuning community means massive upgrade potential. Buy this first.
  2. Nissan 370Z (A-tier, ~₵120,000) — Your drift gateway. Underrated value, strong VQ engine, and parts availability is everywhere. Perfect for learning touge technique while earning credits in drift events.
  3. Subaru WRX STI Rally Car (A-tier, ~₵350,000) — Covers all surface types. Rally suspension eats rough roads alive, AWD provides confidence in any weather, and the 550-650hp range is competitive in every rally event.

This trio handles 80% of the game's content. Everything else is specialist optimization.
