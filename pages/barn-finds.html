Top 12 Barn Find Locations in FH6

Discover hidden treasures across the Japan map — from abandoned lots to mountain garages. Few things in Forza Horizon 6 rival the rush of rolling up to a run-down barn, spotting a weathered shape under a blue tarp, and realizing you've just stumbled onto one of the rarest cars in the game.

Top 12 Barn Find Locations

Each barn find location requires you to drive near the area and complete a discovery challenge. The vehicle inside is yours to keep after restoring. Barn finds are one-time discovery events — once you unlock a location, the car is added to your garage permanently, even if you restore and sell it later. Below are all 12 confirmed locations across the Japan map, organized by terrain type.

🚗 Urban Spots

Tokyo and Osaka's dense city blocks are hiding more than just neon signs and traffic. Abandoned garages, forgotten car ports, and dusty back alleys conceal some of FH6's most coveted machines. Urban barn finds reward players who stray from the main roads and explore side streets, parking structures, and industrial back lanes. Time of day matters — early morning (in-game 5–7 AM) means lighter traffic and easier navigation through narrow alleys.

  • Shibuya Parking Structure — Park at the Shibuya Crossing and head north into the multi-story parking structure off Meiji-Dori. Take the spiral ramp up to the 3rd level, where the concrete opens up to a half-exposed top deck. Look for the faded blue tarp in the far southeast corner, tucked between a concrete pillar and a rusted chain-link partition. Approach close enough and a discovery prompt will appear. Difficulty: ⭐⭐ (the structure is easy to navigate and the tarp is visible from the ramp). Estimated time to find: 2–4 minutes. Tip: The parking structure has multiple entry points — entering from the east side off Sakuragaoka-dori puts you closer to the correct level if you want to skip the spiral ramp climb.
  • Akihabara Back Alley — This one is tucked behind the main Akihabara shopping district, accessible via Chuo-Dori's side streets. Head south from the main pedestrian avenue into the narrow service alley that runs parallel to the elevated train tracks. About 80 meters in, look for a rusted corrugated-metal roll-up door on your right, partially hidden behind stacked export crates from the nearby electronics shops. The barn find marker triggers just before the door. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (the alley network is confusing and the door blends in with surrounding warehouse facades). Estimated time to find: 5–8 minutes. Gotcha: Do not confuse this with the similar-looking garage door further south near the pachinko parlor — that one has no marker. If in doubt, check that the door has a small faded racing stripe decal near the top right corner.

🏔️ Mountain Roads

Japan's legendary touge passes and mountain switchbacks offer some of the most atmospheric barn find locations in FH6. The key is to slow down on the hairpins — what looks like a scenic overlook often hides a small garage or car port. Most mountain barns are unmarked and easy to overshoot at speed, so a careful approach on the throttle is essential. Rainy or foggy weather conditions can make these locations trickier to spot, so consider doing them in clear daylight for best visibility.

  • Hakone Mountain Pass — Drive the Route 1 Hakone bypass until you reach the famous sequence of hairpin turns south of the Hakone Checkpoint. Turn 7 is a tight left hairpin with a stone retaining wall on the outside and a narrow dirt access road on the inside. Pull off the main road onto that dirt track — the small wooden barn is set back about 20 meters, partially obscured by bamboo grove. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ (turn 7 is easy to identify but the dirt access road is easy to miss at speed). Estimated time to find: 3–5 minutes. Tip: There is a small roadside shrine just before turn 7 that serves as a visual cue — when you pass the shrine on your left, start braking for the hairpin. The barn is on the inside of the turn, not the outside.
  • Mount Fuji Scenic Route — The route to Fuji's 5th station along Route 138 is a long and winding climb. About 1.2 kilometers past the Sawaimisaki Rest Stop (on the left-hand side), look for the distinctive red-painted barn with the Forza Horizon diamond logo stencilled on the side wall. It sits on the opposite (east) side of the road from the rest stop, so you will need to cross to the oncoming lane carefully. The barn is set about 30 meters back from the road behind a low stone fence. Difficulty: ⭐⭐ (the red barn is highly visible and the rest stop is a reliable reference point). Estimated time to find: 2–3 minutes. Gotcha: This location is only accessible during daytime in-game. If you arrive at night the marker may not trigger — fast-travel to the rest stop and wait until morning if needed.

🌾 Rural Roads

The Japanese countryside is scattered with traditional farmhouses, old storage sheds, and forgotten agricultural barns that occasionally hide automotive gems. Rural barn finds require the most patience — the roads are narrow, signage is sparse, and many of the structures look similar. Using the minimap to mark the general area before exploring on foot (or at low speed) will save a lot of backtracking. Some rural barns are on private-access dirt roads that may require an off-road-capable vehicle, so consider bringing a rugged drivetrain if you plan to hunt these systematically.

  • Kyoto Countryside — From Kyoto Station, take Route 9 south for approximately 11 kilometers until you reach the small agricultural village of Kumiyama. The barn find is in a traditional Japanese farmhouse compound on the east side of the road, identifiable by its aged red-lacquer front door and a small moss-covered stone lantern beside the entrance. The barn structure is behind the main house, accessed via a dirt footpath. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (the village roads are winding and the farmhouse entrance is easy to confuse with neighboring compounds). Estimated time to find: 6–10 minutes. Tip: Look for the distinctive red door specifically — most other houses in the area have weathered grey or brown doors. If you pass a small family shrine on your left you've gone about 200 meters too far.
  • Nara Park Area — This barn find sits near the eastern edge of Nara Park, behind the Daian-ji Zen temple grounds. Approach from the Nara Park east entrance road and park near the temple gate. Walk (do not drive) about 50 meters northeast from the gate along the dirt path that runs behind the temple's main hall. The storage barn is a low grey wooden structure with a corrugated roof, partially hidden by a line of cedar trees. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ (the temple is a good landmark but the barn itself is not visible from the road). Estimated time to find: 4–7 minutes. Gotcha: The deer in Nara Park are harmless to your car but they can block your path and cause you to stall — do not try to drive through groups of them near the temple area. Leave your car at the road and approach on foot for the cleanest experience.

🌊 Coastal Roads

Japan's coastline is peppered with fishing villages, old harbors, and salt-worn storage sheds where rust and salt air have been slowly claiming forgotten machines for decades. Coastal barn finds often require you to navigate tight harbor roads and narrow waterfront lanes, so a vehicle with good low-speed handling is ideal. The salt air in these areas can give the barns a weathered, oxidized look — use that to your advantage when searching, as the most derelict-looking structures often hold the best surprises.

  • Okinawa Harbor — Drive into the main ferry terminal area of Okinawa Port and look for Warehouse B, a large blue shipping container depot near the terminal gate. Park near the administrative building at the harbor entrance, then proceed on foot toward the blue containers on the north side of Warehouse B. The barn find is concealed behind the third row of containers — specifically behind the teal container marked with white stencil text reading "OKN-07." The blue tarp will be visible once you are within 5 meters. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ (the warehouse area is large and the container rows look similar). Estimated time to find: 5–8 minutes. Tip: The marker only appears when you are on foot or moving slowly — driving through at speed may cause you to miss the prompt. Keep your camera lens pointed between the container rows rather than at the main terminal buildings.
  • Enoshima Beach Access Road — Take the scenic coastal road (Route 134) that runs along the Enoshima waterfront. The barn is on the inland side of the road, about 600 meters north of the Enoshima Aquarium exit. Look for a weathered white wooden garage structure with barely legible racing stripe decals (originally blue and orange, now faded to grey-white) on the upper door panels. It sits behind a low chain-link fence that is partially collapsed on the north side, allowing easy access on foot. Difficulty: ⭐⭐ (the racing stripes are distinctive and the location is on a relatively straight road). Estimated time to find: 2–4 minutes. Gotcha: There is a small parking area almost directly across the road from the barn — many players mistake this for the correct spot. The actual barn is slightly further north (about 40 meters), on the same side as the aquarium, not the parking area.

🏜️ Desert Roads

The northern and central desert plains of FH6's Japan map contain ghost towns, abandoned mining settlements, and lonely farmsteads where time seems to have stopped decades ago. Desert barn finds reward exploration off the main roads — the best strategy is to use the dirt roads and farm access tracks that branch off the main routes. The open terrain and sparse landmarks mean it is easy to overshoot a location, so keep your minimap zoomed in and watch for structural shapes on the horizon. Mid-morning in-game offers the best balance of good visibility and manageable desert traffic.

  • Northern Desert Ghost Town — The abandoned mining town in the northern desert region is one of the most distinctive landmarks on the map. Drive north from the central plains toward the Mesa Ridge area until you spot the cluster of derelict buildings. The barn find is behind the main general store — a one-story corrugated-metal structure with a faded "GENERAL STORE" sign still partially legible on the west face of the building. The car port is immediately behind it, set 15 meters down a narrow dirt track that branches off the main town square. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ (the town is easy to identify but the car port entrance is behind the building and not visible from the main road). Estimated time to find: 3–6 minutes. Tip: The general store's west face has a faded sun-bleached pickup truck illustration on it — use that as your marker and circle behind it to find the port. If you reach the water tower you've gone too far north.
  • Central Plains Dusty Shed — This barn find is on the central plains, along the dusty farm access road that runs east-west between Route 16 and the Northern Road interchange. The shed is identifiable by the large faded number "7" spray-painted in red oxide on the rusted sliding door face. It is set about 40 meters back from the main road behind a low mesquite-fence enclosure, and the blue tarp is visible from the road if you are looking carefully. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (the shed is isolated, easy to miss, and the "7" marking has faded significantly). Estimated time to find: 6–10 minutes. Gotcha: There are several other isolated farm sheds along this road that look similar — they all lack the number "7" marking. If you do not see the "7" painted on the door, it is not the correct shed. Patience is key here.

🎪 Festival Site Roads

Each FH6 festival site has a primary event arena, a tent camping zone, and a surrounding perimeter of service roads, loading docks, and support buildings. Barn finds in festival areas are tucked behind the scenes — in the infrastructure that supports the big events but sits just outside the event fences. These locations tend to be easier to find than their rural counterparts because the festival site road network is well-mapped, but the trade-off is navigating event-related traffic and temporary barriers. Fast-travel to the festival site and start from the nearest road marker to minimize navigation time.

  • Tokyo Festival Site — The Tokyo festival site is a large urban event arena. From the main festival structure, walk or drive past the tent camping area on the north side until you reach the corrugated-metal service barn at the edge of the event zone. The barn is past the last row of tents, sitting on a raised concrete pad. A blue Forza Horizon banner is sometimes (but not always) draped over the structure — do not rely on the banner for identification. Look for the corrugated metal panels and the riveted steel door frame. Difficulty: ⭐⭐ (the barn is in plain sight once you pass the tent camping area). Estimated time to find: 2–3 minutes. Gotcha: During a live festival event, temporary barriers and crowds may block the path. If the festival is active, fast-travel to a nearby road marker and approach from outside the event perimeter instead of through the main gate.
  • Osaka Harbor Festival — The Osaka Harbor Festival site sits adjacent to the port dockyards. From the main festival plaza, head east toward the dock warehouses until you reach the yellow warehouse with a racing flag emblem on its roll-up door — this is the correct building. The barn find is inside the ground floor of this warehouse, through a side access door on the south face (the dock-facing side). The yellow door with the racing flag symbol serves as the visual cue from outside. Difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐ (the warehouse area is large and industrial with many similar-looking yellow doors; the racing flag emblem is the key identifier). Estimated time to find: 4–7 minutes. Tip: The racing flag emblem is a small black-and-white checkered pattern on a yellow diamond background — it is easy to mistake a plain yellow door for the correct one. Look specifically for the checkered flag symbol stencilled at eye level on the door.

Tips & Strategy

Maximize your barn find collection with these strategic guidelines. Barn finds are time-sensitive, sometimes exclusive, and always more rewarding than buying from the Autoshow. Here is everything you need to know to build a complete collection.

When to Do Barn Finds — Early vs. Late Game

There is a widespread misconception that barn finds should be saved for late game. The reality is more nuanced. Some barn find vehicles — particularly the Toyota 2000GT and Nissan Skyline GT-R — have an Autoshow value that makes acquiring them early a significant economic advantage. The rule of thumb: prioritize the S-class and high-value S1 barn finds as early as possible, since the credits you save by not buying them at Autoshow prices compound over the course of a fresh playthrough.

That said, there are also barn finds that reward a late-game approach. The rural and desert locations in particular can be difficult to reach without an off-road or high-clearance vehicle, and some require navigating roads that are only accessible once you have unlocked additional festival sites and fast-travel points. If a barn find location is frustrating to reach, come back after you have expanded your garage with more suitable vehicles — it will not disappear.

How to Restart a Barn Find If You Missed One

Forza Horizon 6 does not have a traditional New Game Plus, but there are two practical paths if you missed a barn find and want to acquire the vehicle:

  • Autoshow availability: Many barn find vehicles — though not all — become available for purchase at the Autoshow after a certain period of game progression. Check the Autoshow under the relevant category before assuming a vehicle is permanently locked. Autoshow vehicles are typically unrestored and full price, so this is the most accessible fallback option.
  • Seasonal Forza Marketplace: Some barn find-exclusive vehicles appear as rewards in limited-time seasonal events on the Forza Horizon festival playlist. These events rotate weekly and some have been known to offer barn find vehicles as Seasonal Championship prizes. Check the Festival Playlist tab each week — if a barn find vehicle appears as a reward, completing the associated championship series will unlock it regardless of your original game state.
  • Start a new Festival Director career: If you are a completionist who genuinely cannot tolerate missing a vehicle, the only guaranteed path to a 100% barn find collection is a fresh save file. Barn find locations are fixed and do not randomize between saves, so on a new playthrough you can plan an efficient route to hit all 12 locations consecutively before doing anything else.

Barn Find Exclusivity vs. Buying from the Autoshow

Not all barn find vehicles are exclusive to the barn find system. Of the 12 vehicles in this guide, approximately 8 of them eventually become available in the Autoshow after you reach the mid-game Horizon Festival Level 20+. The remaining 4 are barn-find-exclusive and can never be purchased — these should be treated as the highest priorities on your collection list:

  • Toyota 2000GT (1967) — exclusive
  • Nissan Skyline GT-R R32 (1992) — exclusive
  • Isuzu 117 Coupé (1968) — exclusive
  • Subaru 360 (1958) — exclusive

The exclusive vehicles are worth prioritizing because they cannot be obtained by any other means after your save file is established. If you miss one of these on a completed save, your only option is the seasonal Marketplace or a fresh save file.

Seasonal Barn Find Events

Forza Horizon 6 introduces a rotating seasonal system that occasionally features barn find vehicles as Festival Playlist rewards. Each week, the Festival Playlist presents a set of challenges across multiple series (Summer, Autumn, Winter, Spring). Some series include a barn find vehicle as their Championship reward, giving you an opportunity to acquire vehicles you may have missed — or duplicates that you can sell to fund restorations.

Additionally, Playground Games seasonal events have been known to feature barn find car meets as their reward structure, where completing a car meet challenge unlocks a barn find vehicle at no additional cost. Keep an eye on the weekly Festival Playlist and complete as many series as possible — the investment in time is far lower than the credits saved by avoiding Autoshow purchases of these rare vehicles.

Pro tip: If you see a barn find vehicle as a seasonal reward and you have already discovered it, check whether the reward is a duplicate. Duplicate barn find vehicles can be sold at the Autoshow for their full value, and the proceeds can fund restoration work on your existing barn find acquisitions. A smart seasonal strategy turns barn finds into a net credit-positive activity.
